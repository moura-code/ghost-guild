# Stage 8 — The Crypt Is Occupied

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: superpowers:executing-plans. Executed
> inline, TDD where a test can see it, one commit per batch.

**Goal:** The stage 7 review fixed everything that is wrong *in a screenshot*. What
is left is wrong *in motion*, and a still frame cannot show it: the game has no
feel. This stage is the "still open" list from stage 7 that does not need an artist.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-29-3d-pivot-design.md`.
**Predecessor:** `2026-08-29-3d-s7-playable.md`.

**Baseline:** 799 tests, 85 suites, green.

## The diagnosis

Not from screenshots this time. Every item below is an **absence**, and an absence
is exactly what a still frame cannot show — which is why stage 7's five-shot method
did not find any of them. These came from reading the frame loop and asking what
happens between frames.

| # | Problem | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your feet make no sound and the camera never moves.** `Player._physics_process` is `move_and_slide()` and nothing else. A perfectly level camera gliding in silence is a flying camera, not a person walking down a corridor. | `game/world/player.gd` |
| 2 | **Every torch is a lamp.** `DungeonBuilder.torch_light` builds an `OmniLight3D` with constant `light_energy`. Fire that does not move is not fire — and torches are the only light in the game, so the entire image is frozen. | `game/world/dungeon_builder.gd` |
| 3 | **All sound is 2D.** Every voice in `Sfx` is an `AudioStreamPlayer`. Nothing in the world makes a sound at all: no torch crackle, no enemy in space. A blow landing across the room sounds exactly like one landing in your face. | `game/theme/sfx.gd` |
| 4 | **Nothing has a tail.** No audio buses, no reverb. Dry blips in a stone corridor read as UI over a picture rather than sound in a place. | `game/theme/sfx.gd`, `project.godot` |
| 5 | **The sound set was authored for a different game.** `tools/audio/generate.py` says so in its own docstring: square waves are "the sonic equivalent of a 28-colour palette at 640x360". That palette is on `main` and is gone from here. Square-wave blips under wet photoreal stone is exactly the coherence failure `ART_BRIEF.md` defines as an asset flip. | `tools/audio/generate.py` |

Ordered worst-first the way stage 7 was: 1 is the one a player would name within
ten seconds of picking up a controller, and 5 is the one they would not be able to
name but would feel as "cheap".

## Batches

### Batch A — feet and head

**Files:** `game/world/stride.gd` (new), `game/world/player.gd`,
`tools/audio/generate.py`, tests.

- `Stride`: a pure module. Distance walked → stride phase → footfall count and a
  head-bob offset. Every part of it is a static function, because the suite cannot
  watch a camera but it can check that two metres at a walk is a specific number of
  footfalls and that the head is lowest exactly when a foot lands.
- Sprinting lengthens the stride rather than only speeding the cadence, so running
  is longer steps and not frantic feet.
- Bob amplitude eases in and out with speed: standing still is a still camera, and
  stopping does not freeze the head mid-dip.
- Three stone footstep samples, chosen round-robin with a pitch jitter, so a
  corridor does not machine-gun one sample.

### Batch B — the fire moves

**Files:** `game/world/flame.gd` (new), `game/world/dungeon_builder.gd`,
`game/world/player.gd`, tests.

- `Flame`: pure flicker. Summed sines at incommensurable rates (not `randf()`, so
  it is testable and reproducible) → an energy multiplier that stays inside a
  stated band. Each torch gets its own phase offset from its position, so twenty
  torches never pulse in unison — which is the tell that makes flicker look like a
  broken light rather than a flame.
- The hero's own torch flickers too, at a different rate, because it is closer and
  a matching rhythm would read as the whole room strobing.

### Batch C — sound has a place

**Files:** `game/theme/sfx.gd`, `game/world/crawl.gd`, `game/fight/*`, tests.

- Real buses: `Sfx` and `Ambience` under `Master`, with a reverb on the world path.
  Built in code at boot so `project.godot` stays the one file nobody hand-edits.
- Torch crackle as `AudioStreamPlayer3D` on each flame, so the room's own sound
  arrives from where the fire is.
- Blows play at the enemy that took them.

### Batch D — the voice matches the stone

**Files:** `tools/audio/generate.py`, `assets/sfx/*`.

- The synthesis stays (no licence, no download, deterministic). The **voice**
  changes: noise bodies through a resonant filter instead of square waves, longer
  tails, impacts with a transient and a body rather than a beep.

## Self-review

Items 1→A, 2→B, 3/4→C, 5→D. The tests can see: stride maths, bob geometry, flicker
bounds and de-phasing, bus layout, and — because physics and `Input.action_press`
both work headless — that really walking a real `Player` really emits footfalls.
What no test can see is whether it *sounds* right; that is stated as a limit rather
than asserted, the same way stage 7 stated it about colour.

## Executed — batches A and B

**835 tests, 88 suites, green.** `core/` is untouched: the diff contains no file
under it, which is the whole reason the three demos need no re-baselining.

| Review item | What changed |
|---|---|
| **1 · silent feet, level camera** | `Stride` — distance in, footfalls and a head-bob offset out, twelve tests. Driven by ground actually covered rather than by the clock, so walking into a wall stops the feet. Sprinting lengthens the stride instead of only raising the cadence. The head is at its LOWEST on the footfall, which is the half of a bob that is usually backwards. `Player.footfall` is a signal, so the body never learns what a footstep sounds like and the suite can assert the cadence with no audio server. Three stone samples in rotation with a pitch jitter. |
| **2 · every torch was a lamp** | `Flame` — bounded, deterministic, summed sines at rates with no common period, phase taken from where the torch stands so neighbouring brackets never breathe together. `Torches` (the group `DungeonBuilder` was already building) drives them, so it is one `_process` per floor and it dies with the floor. The hero's own torch runs slower and out of phase: on the wall rate, a light a hand's length from the camera reads as the room strobing. |

### The pre-existing crash this found

`tools\test.cmd tests` exits **139 (segfault)** after GdUnit4 prints its own
`Exit code: 0` and all tests pass. Verified pre-existing by running the full suite
in a clean worktree at `fbf8d60`: identical — 799/799 green, then 139. It is
teardown, after the report is written, and it does not reproduce on a subset. The
criterion for this suite is therefore the runner's summary, not the shell's exit
code.

## Executed — batch C

**857 tests, 89 suites, green.**

| Review item | What changed |
|---|---|
| **4 · nothing had a tail** | Three buses built in code at boot — `Ui` (dry), `World` (an `AudioEffectReverb` tuned for stone, not a cathedral: 26 ms predelay, 0.24 wet, because every sound here is a short transient and reverb on a transient is audible at a fraction of what a sustained sound needs) and `Ambience` (dry, it is already a room). `Sfx.bus_for` is pure, so the routing is assertable — a bus name that names no bus is routed to Master silently, which means the sound still plays and only the reverb is missing. |
| **3 · all sound was 2D** | Every torch carries an `AudioStreamPlayer3D` with a six-second crackle loop, started at its own offset in the loop — position-derived plus a golden-ratio step per torch, because position alone is a hash and a hash is allowed to put two of them next to each other. Audible for four cells, so a corridor is walked past one torch at a time. `Voices3D` puts blows and deaths where the creature is standing, with a fallback to the flat pool for a body that has already been freed. Being hit yourself stays flat: you are the listener. |

### Two bugs that only the wiring found

- Setting `player.bus` to a bus that does not exist yet does not fail — Godot keeps
  a garbled name (the test read back `AmbiMastencer`) and plays dry on Master. Every
  pool now calls `ensure_buses()` before it builds a player.
- `DungeonBuilder.build` reported `torches.get_child_count()`, which doubled the
  moment every torch got a voice. `Torches.count()` now answers the question that
  was actually being asked.
