# M2 Stage 7 — Legends

Spec §6.1, §4.4, §5.4. The prestige layer, the Blessing, and the reason to
reach floor 30 twice.

## Why this, now

The dungeon is thirty floors deep and the game has no answer to reaching the
bottom. There is no reason to start again, no reward for having been deep, and
`record_depth` — which the code has carried since M1 and which §6.1 says is the
thing that persists — has never had anything to persist across.

It is also the missing half of the power curve. §4.4 is explicit: without the
Blessing "hero power caps at roughly 7× while enemies at floor 60 have 33× HP".
The Kiln already shows the shape of that — a played-in Sexton holds 0.64 at
floor 24 and 0.18 at floor 30 — and there is nothing in the game that would
ever make floor 40 possible.

## What a Legend is

All your ghosts merged into one memorial. §6.1:

- Unlocked when a true ghost stands on floor 15; the threshold rises 15 per
  cycle, so the first prestige lands in the first few hours.
- Every ghost merges — true, expedition, echo — into a **mass**.
- The Legend's multiplier is `1 + k × sqrt(mass / M0)`, applied to ghost
  strength *and* to hero damage and block. That is the Blessing (§4.4).
- A **trait** from the dominant archetype: the most common card tag across the
  merged decks.
- **The Hall keeps every merged epitaph.** A Legend is a memorial, not a
  deletion, and this is the emotional argument of the whole feature: the run
  where you died on floor 22 is still written down.
- Kept: reach, unlocks, biome claims, upgrades, Legends, Ink. Reset: the
  ladder (a new Founder on floor 1), Soul, the waypoint.

## Batches

### Batch A — the maths

**Files:** `core/legends/legend.gd` (new), `core/legends/legends.gd` (new),
`data/balance.json`, tests.

`Legend` is a record: id, name, cycle, mass, multiplier, trait, the epitaphs it
keeps, created_at. `Legends` is the arithmetic: the threshold for a cycle, the
mass of a ladder, the multiplier, the dominant archetype, and the **combined**
blessing of every Legend the guild has.

Pure and total: a guild with no Legends has a blessing of exactly 1.0, so
every number in the game is unchanged until the player prestiges. That is the
identity property this codebase builds everything to.

### Batch B — the rite

**Files:** `core/campaign.gd`, `core/campaign_engine.gd`, `core/save/`, tests.

`CampaignEngine.prestige` — refuses below the threshold, refuses mid-run,
merges, keeps what §6.1 says to keep and resets what it says to reset. The save
round-trips Legends and Ink.

The riskiest line in the stage is the keep/reset list, because getting it wrong
either erases a player's progress or makes prestige free. It is asserted field
by field.

### Batch C — the Blessing

**Files:** `core/combat/fight_state.gd`, `core/combat/effect_resolver.gd`,
`core/ghosts/`, `core/run/`, tests.

One multiplier on the fight, applied to hero damage dealt and block gained, and
the same multiplier on every ghost's strength. It has to reach `FightState`,
which means it has to reach every simulation too — the autopilot, projections,
expeditions — or a ghost would be priced without the Blessing that it fights
with.

Default 1.0 everywhere, so the demos are byte-identical.

### Batch D — the Hall

**Files:** `game/hud/hall_screen.gd` (new), `game/world/guild_room.gd`,
`data/strings/en.csv`, tests, one rendered shot.

The Hall of Legends: what each Legend is worth, what trait it carries, and the
epitaphs it keeps. Plus the rite itself — a screen that tells you exactly what
you are about to lose and what you are about to keep, because the one thing a
prestige button must never do is surprise someone.

## Self-review

The failure mode for a prestige layer is that it is a button that makes numbers
bigger. What stops that here is the Hall: the ghosts do not vanish, they become
a named thing with their epitaphs attached, and §6.1's own sentence — "the
Legend is a memorial, not a deletion" — is the whole design.

The mechanical risk is the Blessing reaching some simulations and not others.
A ghost priced without the Blessing that it fights with would make every yield
number in the game quietly wrong, in the direction of under-reporting, which is
the direction nobody notices. So the multiplier travels on the snapshot rather
than being looked up, and there is a test that a blessed hero and a blessed
ghost move together.

## Executed

All four batches.

### Batch A — the maths

`Legend` and `Legends`, both pure. `blessing([])` is exactly 1.0, so every
number and every demo in the repo is unchanged until a player prestiges — the
identity property this codebase builds everything to.

`multiplier` is `1 + k·sqrt(mass/M0)` with k = 0.5 and M0 = 400, and the test
that matters is the shape rather than the constants: quadrupling the mass
doubles what the Legend adds, so a tenth cycle is worth about three times a
first rather than a thousand times.

The trait is the most common card tag across the merged decks, ties broken
alphabetically — it goes on a permanent record and must not depend on
dictionary order. The Legend is named after the deepest of its dead.

### Batch B — the rite

`CampaignEngine.prestige`, with the keep/reset list written out in the
docstring rather than spread across the code, and asserted field by field.

**The one that would have been a silent disaster:** biome claims are *derived
from the ladder*, and the rite wipes the ladder. A naive prestige would
therefore have re-locked the Fungal Deep — and taken the Hexer with it — as a
reward for reaching floor 15. `Campaign.claimed_biomes` banks them before the
merge and `Biomes.claimed` reads the union, which meant threading a `banked`
argument through `Biomes.claimed`, `claimed_pools` and `Classes.unlocked`.

Expeditions in the field are dropped rather than kept: one landing into the
next cycle would carry the last cycle's strength into a wiped ladder.

### Batch C — the Blessing

`blessing` travels on `HeroSnapshot`, which is the one object every path that
starts a fight already passes — the live run, the autopilot's clones, the
next-floor projection, an echo's pricing, an expedition's survival check and
its strength. Nothing has to remember to look it up, which is what the plan
called the real risk: a ghost priced without the Blessing it fights with makes
every yield in the game quietly low, and low is the direction nobody notices.

`EffectResolver.blessed` is the identity at 1.0 by construction — integer in,
integer out, no rounding drift — and the three demos are byte-identical.

It multiplies damage and block only. Not statuses (§4.4 names damage and block)
and not the enemy's damage, which would cancel out; both are asserted.

### Batch D — the Hall

A fifth station in the guild, and a screen whose first job is to say what the
rite takes before it is pressed: the exact number of dead and the exact Soul,
in warning red, at the same weight as the reward. The cost line is hidden while
the button is disabled, because a warning about something you cannot do yet is
noise.

The Hall lists every Legend with its epitaphs at body weight rather than as
fine print. That is the feature: §6.1 says "the Legend is a memorial, not a
deletion", and a list of multipliers would be a spreadsheet of the people who
died for them.

The render caught one layout bug immediately — `ScreenLayout.section` expands
to fill, so added straight to a screen its rule ran the full 1280 pixels while
the plates under it sat in a 450-pixel column, and the heading detached from
the thing it headed.

### Notes for later

- A Legend's trait is recorded and shown and does not yet *do* anything. §6.1
  wants a Poison Legend to make the hero's Poison cards apply +1 stack, and
  §6.1 also wants one Legend invocable per run as an extra relic. Both are
  combat hooks and belong with the next combat stage rather than bolted on
  here.
- Ink accrues and has nothing to spend it on until the Chronicle (§6.2, five
  Legends).
