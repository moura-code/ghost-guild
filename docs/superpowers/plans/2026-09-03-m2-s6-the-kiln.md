# M2 Stage 6 — The Kiln

Spec §2, §7. The third biome, floors 21–30, constructs and fire — and the
stage that makes the affinity table mean something in every direction.

## Why this, now

Three biomes is the launch depth (§11). Two of the three exist, the third has
an accent authored in `Palette.BIOME_ACCENTS` that nothing reads, and
`Kit.BIOME_STONE` has a fallback waiting for it.

The sharper reason is balance. There are two classes and the Deep made one of
them the deep-diving class:

| | Sexton | Hexer |
|---|---|---|
| Catacombs | ahead | behind |
| Fungal Deep | behind | ahead |

So the Hexer is currently the answer to every floor past ten, and "the second
class you unlock is the one you play from then on" is the failure mode the
Hexer stage was written to avoid, arriving one biome later. The Kiln is where
Poison stops working — `affinity.json` already says Poison does **×0** into
Construct — and the Sexton comes back.

## What the Kiln is

Constructs and fire. Where the Catacombs hit hard and the Deep wears you down,
the Kiln **burns and holds**: high block, `burn` on the hero, and bodies that
have to be broken rather than out-lasted.

Three claims, all measurable:

1. Poison is worth nothing there, so a Hexer starting deck must do measurably
   worse in the Kiln than in the Deep.
2. The Sexton must be the better class in the Kiln.
3. Each of the three biomes must therefore have a different best class or a
   different best archetype — which is the thing that makes a dungeon a
   journey rather than a list of floors.

## Batches

### Batch A — the biome

**Files:** `data/biomes/the_kiln.json`, `data/enemies/the_kiln.json`,
`data/cards/the_kiln.json`, `data/affinity.json`, `data/strings/en.csv`,
icons, tests.

Ten enemies tagged Construct and Fire, ten cards in the `the_kiln` pool, three
encounter bands over floors 21–30, an elite and a boss.

`affinity.json` grows two rows: `bone` ×1.25 into Construct (blunt force works
on a thing made of stone) and `ember` ×0.6 into it (you cannot burn a kiln).
Poison ×0 into Construct is already there and has never mattered.

### Batch B — the stone

**Files:** `assets/materials/` (new CC0 sets), `game/world/kit.gd`,
`ATTRIBUTION.md`, tests, renders.

Fired brick and scorched metal, hot where the Deep is cold. Judged by opening
the colour maps, the way the Deep's were.

### Batch C — thirty floors

**Files:** `game/widgets/tower_view.gd`, `game/world/well_view.gd`, tests,
renders.

The tower and the well are both sized to the dungeon and were last looked at
at twenty. Thirty rows in 360 logical pixels is twelve pixels a floor, which
is smaller than the numbers written in them.

## Self-review

The risk in Batch A is the one the Hexer stage already hit: a biome that is
harder rather than different. The guard is the same crossed comparison, run
across all three biomes at once — every class must be the best one somewhere,
and the table above must have no row that wins everywhere.

The risk in Batch C is that thirty floors do not fit and the fix is to make
the tower scroll, which would cost it the one thing it is for: §9 calls the
ladder the capsule image, and a capsule you scroll is not one.

## Executed

All three batches, plus a bug the render found that had been shipping for three
stages.

### Batch A — the biome

Ten enemies, ten cards, three encounter bands, twenty icons. `affinity.json`
gained `bone` ×1.25 into Construct and `ember` ×0.6 into it. An `ember` ×1.25
into Flesh row was written and then removed: it was not needed for anything the
Kiln claims and it changed damage on floors that already exist, which the demos
would have caught and which is not this stage's business.

The crossed comparison, on starting decks, same seed, 40 fights:

| floor | biome | Sexton | Hexer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Catacombs | **0.92** | 0.84 |
| 14 | Fungal Deep | 0.40 | **0.72** |
| 21 | The Kiln | **0.36** | 0.00 |

Every class is the best one somewhere, which is the property
`test_every_class_is_the_best_one_somewhere` asserts and the reason the Kiln
exists: after the Deep, the Hexer was the answer to every floor past ten.

The comparison floor for the Kiln is 21 rather than 24, because by 24 neither
class clears a fight with ten cards and two zeroes are not a contrast. With a
played-in hero (6 Might, 6 Wit, 4 Vigor) the Sexton holds 0.64 at floor 24 and
0.18 at floor 30, and the Hexer is at zero from floor 24 — its starting deck's
main verb does nothing to a construct, which is the design, and drafting is the
answer the game already provides.

### Batch B — the stone

Bricks056 (dark vitrified brick) for the walls, Rock020 for the floor, Rock035
(near-black, veined) for the ceiling. Lava004 was rejected on sight: a floor of
molten rock would make the whole frame orange, and the game's rule is that
colour comes from the lighting.

**The Kiln's tints stay cool, like every other biome's.** The first draft
tinted them warm — it is the fire biome, after all — and that quietly broke
`test_the_stone_is_grey_so_the_light_can_be_the_colour`, which is the rule that
makes a torch read as fire rather than as a brightness setting. The heat comes
from the torches and from the biome's accent in the fog. The test now runs
across every biome rather than only the default one, so the next biome cannot
break it either.

### Batch C — thirty floors

`TowerView.numbered` labels every floor while the shaft is twenty or shorter
and every fifth after that. Thirty rows in 360 logical pixels is twelve pixels
a row against an eleven-pixel line of text, and the render was one unreadable
column of collided digits. It is a scale now: 1, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, with
three colour bands behind it — which is the capsule image §9 asks for.

### What the render found: every card with a number in it was lying

The Kiln shot landed on a descent draft and showed a card reading **"Gain {0}
Block and {1} Thorns."**

Nothing in the game substituted card-text placeholders. The Fungal Deep, the
Hexer and the Kiln were all authored assuming something did, so **thirty-two
cards had shipped with raw placeholders on their faces** across three stages,
and no test saw it: `test_every_key_the_content_names_resolves` checks that a
key resolves, not that what it resolves to means anything.

Chasing it turned up an older one. The original cards spell their numbers into
the string — "Gain 11 Block." — and nothing rewrites that for an upgrade, so
**every upgraded card in the game had always been wrong about itself.** Bone
Wall+ grants 18 Block and said 11.

`CardText` takes the numbers out of the effects, in authored order, and the
string carries only the words. All sixty-five cards are templated: thirty were
converted mechanically, each verified by substituting back and requiring the
original string byte for byte. The guard is
`test_a_card_whose_numbers_change_says_so` — if an upgrade changes what a card
does, the card has to read differently — which is the precise version of "no
number is written into the string" and does not false-positive on Hallowed
Strike's "50% more against the undead".

### Two more the suite found once the dungeon was thirty deep

- **The well stopped at twenty.** `WellView.MAX_FLOORS` was 20 — the dungeon's
  depth on the day it was written — so the shaft in the guild silently stopped
  showing the ghosts standing in the biome the player had just unlocked. It is
  a guard against corrupt content now (60) rather than a design limit, with a
  test that ties it to `Biomes.depth`.
- **"Breaking the Mould" did not fit on a card.** `card_view_test` measures
  every name against the card's own name field and caught it immediately.
  Renamed to Sunder rather than shrunk: a name that only fits at a smaller
  size is a name that stops fitting again.
