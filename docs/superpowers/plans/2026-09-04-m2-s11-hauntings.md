# M2 Stage 11 — Hauntings

Spec §5.6. The reason to put your dead together.

## What the spec asks for

"A floor with three or more ghosts sharing an archetype forms a Haunting:
+15% spawn_rate per matching ghost, capped at +75%. Synergy raises the floor's
**cap** — 'a bigger floor' — rather than each ghost's strength."

That last clause is the whole design, and the other reading is the obvious one.
Buffing each ghost's strength would be worth almost nothing on a floor that is
already saturated — which is exactly the floor the player has been stacking
their dead on. Raising the spawn rate raises the ceiling the strength is
measured against, so a haunting pays **most** where the dead are thickest.
That is the mechanic that says *put your Poison dead together* instead of
*spread them thin*, and there is a test named after it.

## Where it lives

`Hauntings` is pure and takes `(content, ladder, floor, balance)`. It cannot
live in `Ladder`, which holds ghosts and has never needed to know what a card
is — and a ghost's archetype is the tag its deck carries most of.

So hauntings ride in with the other floor modifiers: `Campaign.modifiers()`
has the content and builds a `{floor: multiplier}` table, and `Ladder` gains
one lookup, `spawn_mult`. Every existing caller of `floor_output`,
`saturation` and `marginal_yield` picks the feature up without changing.

A ghost's archetype is worked out on demand and cached on the ghost. Not saved
and not a constructor argument: a deck never changes after the ghost is made,
and the three constructors take a `Hero` rather than the `Content` it would
take to read a card's tags. The cache matters because `modifiers()` asks every
ghost on the ladder, and that is a path the Soul counter walks ten times a
second.

## Two things worth naming

**The demos are byte-identical.** Hauntings change the yield of any floor with
three matching dead on it, and the balance sim never puts three on a floor —
its marginal-yield probe places one candidate at a time. So the invariants are
measuring the same thing they always were, and a haunting is entirely a
player-driven choice rather than a silent rebalance.

**A comma in an unquoted CSV value ends the value.** The haunting line shipped
for an afternoon reading "Floor 4: 3 Bone" and never what it was worth,
because `ui.haunting` had a comma in it and the loader keeps the half in front.
Nothing errors, no key is missing, and the sentence simply stops. Half an hour
went into the layout before the string was suspected — so
`slice_content_test` now reads `en.csv` raw and fails on any unquoted value
carrying a comma. It found no others.

## Self-review

The risk in the arithmetic was picking the wrong reading of "raises the cap",
and the defence is a test that asserts a haunting on a saturated floor pays
more than the same haunting on an empty one — which is false under the
strength reading and true under this one.

The risk in the UI was that a floor quietly paying 45% more teaches nobody
anything. The tower colours a haunted floor's rate amber; the line under the
shaft names the floor, the archetype and the number, and when there is no
haunting it says how to make one.
